Professor Rangaswamy Narasimhan (1926–2007)

Biographical note

A pioneering computer and cognitive scientist, Narasimhan is widely regarded as the father of computer science research in India and a central architect of the country’s early computing ecosystem.

Trained first in telecommunication engineering at the University of Madras, he went to the United States on a Tata Fellowship, earning an MS in Electrical Engineering from Caltech and a PhD in Mathematics from Indiana University in 1954. At Homi J. Bhabha’s invitation, he returned to India that year and joined TIFR in Bombay.

At TIFR he led the design and construction of the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research Automatic Calculator—TIFRAC—India’s first indigenous electronic digital computer. Operational around 1960, it was used by scientists and engineers and introduced many researchers to programming. Narasimhan later emphasised that an important outcome of the project was the spread of computer consciousness among research scientists and the development of specialists in logical, circuit, system, and engineering design.

Research contributions and intellectual vision

Narasimhan’s 1964 paper, “Labeling schemata and syntactic description of pictures,” was an early contribution to syntactic pattern recognition. It explored how ideas from formal language could describe and analyse visual structure. In 1972, “Visual Pattern Analysis in Machines and Animals” connected picture description and parallel processing with questions in visual perception.

Throughout his career, he advocated computational modelling as a methodological tool for the study of agentive behaviour, viewing computers not only as machines but also as instruments for understanding language, perception, and cognition.
Institution building and national impact

He played a central role in establishing TIFR’s National Centre for Software Development and Computing Techniques, later the National Centre for Software Technology and eventually part of C-DAC. He helped establish the Computer Maintenance Corporation, serving as its founding Chairman, and founded the Computer Society of India as its first President.

He advised the Government of India’s Department of Electronics and served as the Indian representative on the Council of the International Federation for Information Processing during 1975–86.
